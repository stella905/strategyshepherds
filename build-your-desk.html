The Modern Communications Desk

Build a communications function that can stay lean without losing intelligence.

Communications teams are getting smaller while the number of channels, stakeholders and demands they manage keeps growing. The answer is not simply more content or more AI. It is a better system that retains organisational knowledge, increases capacity and makes it easier to bring in specialist support when needed.

Build Your Communications DeskTake the Desk Assessment

The communications team of the future is modular.

You do not need every capability permanently in house. You need to retain the things the organisation should never lose, including strategy, institutional knowledge, evidence, relationships, judgement and what the team has learned.

Once that foundation exists, writers, designers, filmmakers, researchers, creators and other specialists can plug into the work when needed. A shared knowledge hub allows them to understand the organisation quickly instead of reconstructing years of context every time a new project begins.

Seven parts of the Desk

A system for how the team listens, learns and makes expertise travel.

The Desk is not another content calendar. It is the infrastructure that helps a communications function hold context, decide what matters, use AI responsibly, collaborate with flexible talent and preserve continuity.

01

Strategy

The team connects communication activity to the outcomes the organisation needs to move, including funding, policy, partnerships, trust, talent, participation and agenda influence.

02

Intelligence

The team has a repeatable way to track the research, policy, funding, media, peer organisations, people and emerging issues that shape the organisation’s field and create communication opportunities.

03

Knowledge and evidence

The organisation’s research, programme learning, case studies, expert insight, previous campaigns and useful story material are organised so that staff, consultants and AI workflows can draw from what the organisation already knows.

04

AI workflows

The team uses AI to increase capacity for research, synthesis, briefing, knowledge retrieval, meeting preparation, repurposing and content operations while keeping accuracy, strategy and accountability human.

05

Flexible capacity and human networks

The team knows which experts, journalists, creators, partners, communities, consultants and specialist creatives can help ideas travel, and it has enough context and briefing infrastructure to bring those people into the work efficiently.

06

Distribution

The organisation coordinates owned, earned, shared, human and paid communication around the ideas that matter most rather than treating every channel as a separate activity.

07

Learning and continuity

The team measures what communication helps change, records what it learns and preserves the relationships, workflows, evidence and context that the next staff member or consultant will need.

The aim is continuity.

If a communications manager leaves, a consultant finishes or a project pauses, the organisation should not lose the context that made the work possible. A good Desk makes communications less dependent on individual memory.

Build organisational memory

Do not make every new consultant start from zero.

The person who knows the journalists leaves. A programme manager changes roles. A consultant finishes. The research was published three years ago. Nobody remembers which presentation contains the useful statistic. These are not only filing problems. They are communication capacity problems.

A modern communications function needs to preserve context so that the next person can begin from what the organisation already knows. That is why knowledge, continuity and consultant onboarding sit at the centre of the Desk.
